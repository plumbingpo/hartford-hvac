These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that ignites and shuts off, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Two situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can save you from a far bigger repair.

Keeping Your Stamford HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
